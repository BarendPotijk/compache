What the existing runs measured

These exploratory Luna runs used two adapted long conversations per benchmark. Compache’s fit used the separate synthetic RAG corpus. Each row prices actual model calls under a shared simulated return schedule. The five-minute window is hypothetical for Luna.

All three policies preserved original source sets for 2 of 19 assessed GoodAI dependencies. On MemoryArena, Compache and OpenCode preserved 19 of 42, and Headroom preserved 21. Every policy missed both MemoryArena final answers. The host still summarized at overflow even though the fitted Compache policy admitted no custom cut.

Why MemoryArena cost more at 30 minutes

About 92% of the $0.247 difference came from tool follow-ups. Compache used 70 tool rounds against Headroom’s 49. In the first conversation, 41 of Compache’s 45 rounds preceded the first host summary. Across both conversations, 51 Compache retrievals and 44 Headroom retrievals requested IDs absent from the conversation so far. The host’s tool could only reread previously introduced documents. In the second conversation Compache used four fewer rounds and cost about $0.013 less.

Compache’s higher cache hit rate (77.8% versus 71.7%) coexisted with 11.7 million more input tokens processed. At five minutes, Compache instead used 32 rounds and cost 3.8% less per turn than Headroom. These are single trajectories on each trace. They establish where the money went. They cannot isolate how much the planner contributed to the model choosing another lookup.

Which earlier results can be reused?
  • Benchmark-specific fits. Eight other conversations from each public benchmark supplied the fitted dependency and timing profiles. Those runs address adaptation to a familiar family. Both benchmarks have since informed development, including the ping policy.
  • Short GoodAI. Four of six scored probes reused a full training script and its target. Training questions recurred on 25 of 37 turns. Exclude those results from independent quality claims.
  • Short MemoryArena. No training question recurred in 14 turns, but a retrieved passage did. The matched Terra/Sol sample had no host overflow summaries and no turns with archived sources. Each policy and model answered one of the two final questions correctly.
  • Frozen checkpoints. The 24 Terra/Sol calls cost $39.23. Each model answered one question on a prior Luna context with recovery disabled. All calls recorded zero cache-read tokens. These data measure evidence available to a first answer and its price. Earlier compaction, ping and recovery costs lie outside that measurement.

Specify the workload before choosing a dataset

Compache chooses among keeping history, compacting eligible content, and refreshing a cached prefix. Each choice needs competing futures. A user may return while the prefix is warm, after expiry, or never. Further turns may add enough context to force overflow or require an earlier source that a cut removed. Fit those distributions on training traces, then test the frozen choices across all selected evaluation threads.

Represent research and support as prospective workload strata. Research can supply larger retrievals and longer returns; support can supply shorter interactions and quick follow-ups. The actual distributions, dependence on prior turns and terminal rate must come from the fit or from an explicitly declared simulation. A topic label supplies none of those measurements.

Investigate the admission failure first. In the two synthetic fitting folds, retaining ten input groups, ten output groups and three retrieval groups kept only about 51% of assessed requirements. Retaining all prior input and output groups and ten retrieval groups reached 100% on assessed requirements but changed context on more than 83% of unassessable turns. The configured gate allowed zero such changes. Audit missing labels, compare finer typed recipes on development traces, and retain keep-all whenever no candidate passes the existing 95% requirement.

Freeze the comparison

  1. Separate training, development and external evaluation. Fit timing, growth and candidate cuts on separate traces grouped by company, source collection or scenario. Choose settings on development data. Record every benchmark family that influenced the implementation. Audit whole scripts, target pairs, case lineage, document hashes and normalized questions across splits. Include derivative tasks in the same family.
  2. Register the workload and endpoints. Freeze dataset revisions, case selection rule, scoring rubrics, study scope, practical quality margin, sample size or precision target, and handling of unknown evidence and failures. Derive these choices from development work. Preserve qualifying no-op threads and cheap-baseline cases. Publish coverage and exclusions irrespective of the sign of savings.
  3. Freeze runtime inputs. Record code, policy and configuration hashes for 5- and 30-minute windows, model identifier and effort, prices, host budget and output reserve, system and tool schemas, cache-key scope, clock seed, retrieval ordering, recovery limits and terminal observation horizon. Keep references, future turns and dependency labels out of inference. Replace grader-only fields with dummy values and verify that serving request hashes are unchanged.
  4. Run paired offline replays. Hold exogenous questions, source packets, scripted answers, tool calls and return schedules fixed. Evaluate the host default, fitted caching alone, fitted cuts alone and both together. Allow planner actions to alter only the eligible context and maintenance schedule. If a proposed replay needs a summary or response absent from the evidence, specify its estimate or leave that event unresolved. This stage measures direct policy economics and source visibility.
  5. Then evaluate the whole agent. After a separate spending decision, let each arm generate its own answers and recovery calls under the same fixture, model and clock. Isolate cache namespaces, balance execution order and replicate by complete thread within budget. Keep actual receipts distinct from virtual expiry replays. Compare live latency on equivalent execution conditions. Record every retry, unused ping and tool failure, including runs with no final answer.

Make the host comparison interpretable

Pin the OpenCode overflow trigger and summary prompt. Document the Headroom handler, version, mode and compression setting. The previous experiment used compression off to isolate caching and compaction. OpenClaw has a separate calculator scenario; the historical public benchmark control was OpenCode. Give each arm the same retriever, documents, page limits and ability to recover introduced evidence. Register Compache’s actual reopen tool; log its schema tokens, results and model follow-ups. Record control differences when exact parity is impossible.

On development fixtures, verify that the query asks for an operation the tools support. A corpus search and a reread-by-ID are different operations. Test both existing and missing IDs, make available IDs discoverable in the model-visible payload, and apply the same failure handling across the comparison. Keep tool rounds in whole-agent cost. Use the fixed-work replay to establish which part of a live difference is attributable to cache and compaction decisions.

How to score the result

Cost per user run
Sum the costs of foreground generation, all tool continuations, retries, summaries and maintenance, then divide by the number of user turns in the matched workload. Define attempted and completed runs before evaluation, disclose both, and reconcile failed-run spend. Report absolute USD/run alongside relative savings, computed as 1 − Compache cost ÷ comparator cost on the same denominator.
Receipt accounting
Deduplicate actual provider response IDs. Apply per-request model rates to disjoint fresh input, cache writes, cache reads and billable output, including any long-context tier. Reprice the same token usage on each logical clock only when the request sequence is valid for that condition. Mark estimated prefix availability and hypothetical windows; keep actual provider spend in a separate ledger.
Dependencies kept
At the first answer request, count a historical requirement as retained when every source in at least one accepted evidence set is present. Divide by the assessed historical requirements. Show numerator, denominator, unknowns, source type and lag. Score successful recovery separately. A fact repeated in a summary can improve answer quality without preserving its original source.
Answer quality
Use a frozen native task grader or blinded semantic graders with explicit references, abstention and adjudication rules. Separate intermediate and final targets. Test the prespecified acceptable quality difference. The policy’s 95% training retention requirement does not imply 95% test-set accuracy.
Latency and rounds
Report foreground wall time and summed foreground API time per user turn, with median and upper quantile. Separate idle time and background pings. Count tool rounds, tool calls, nonexistent IDs, duplicates, successful recoveries, exhausted turns, summaries, discretionary warm/cold cuts and mid-run overflow events.
Cache and uncertainty
State exactly which calls enter the hit rate. Compute cached-read tokens ÷ input tokens for those calls and show both counts. Compare paired thread totals and aggregate cost per turn; quantify uncertainty by resampling whole threads or independent source scenarios. Calendar replicas and repeated turns within one thread do not create new independent conversations.

What to leave for the next decision

  1. Produce an offline inventory. List revisions, licensing, prior exposure, independent thread counts, source types, positive and unknown dependencies, raw context growth and return-time provenance. Deliver the selection rule and overlap report before scoring a candidate policy.
  2. Show that the mechanism is testable on development data. Report eligible recipes and the reasons for rejected ones. Exercise a real discretionary cut and an eligible cache decision on distinct development scenarios. Include terminal, already-warm, broken-prefix and high-dependence controls. A policy that correctly declines action is a valid outcome.
  3. Review the dry run. Attach the frozen protocol, a matched dry-run ledger, invariant checks, hidden-label audit, and expected token/cost bounds. Verify equal schedules and source access, deterministic no-change replay, exactly-once cost accounting, and that recovery and overflow tests reach the intended paths.
  4. Decide whether a live study is warranted. If the source labels cannot support retention scoring, report that limitation. If natural traces have no pressure, publish them as a low-pressure control; label any controlled stress adaptation separately. If no training cut passes, record a cache-only result or revise the candidate set on development data before freezing again. Preserve all losing, neutral and no-action cases.
  5. Hand over the audit trail. Provide artifact hashes, the fitting manifest, complete per-thread measurements, confidence intervals, sample coverage and expense ledger. Keep raw answer text and licensed source data in their permitted locations. A policy change after inspecting external outcomes moves that suite into development for the next revision.

The next deliverable is an offline feasibility report and a frozen protocol. A fresh external performance claim depends on that design surviving the data, including the possibility that simply keeping the host policy is cheaper.
